A Walk Through the Old Harbour

By Jane Writer

The old harbour wakes up slowly. Long before the first tourists arrive, the fishing boats are already coming back in, their decks stacked with crates and their crews tired but cheerful after a night out on the water. Gulls circle overhead, waiting for scraps, and the smell of salt and diesel hangs over the quay.

By seven o'clock the fish market is in full swing. Traders shout prices across the concrete floor, buyers from the town's restaurants poke at the catch, and an old man in a flat cap sells coffee from a cart he has pushed along the same route for thirty years. Nobody seems to be in a hurry, and yet everything gets sold before nine.

"Thirty years, and I have never once been late," says the coffee seller.

Further along the waterfront, the warehouses that once stored grain and timber have been turned into studios, cafés and a small museum. The museum's best exhibit is a hand-drawn map of the harbour from 1820, which shows how much of today's town was still open water two centuries ago. The museum is open daily* (*except Mondays).

What to see

Things worth seeing if you visit:

  • The fish market, early in the morning.
  • The 1820 harbour map in the museum.
  • The lighthouse at the end of the north pier.
  • Tip: go early, before the crowds.

Getting there:

  1. Take the coastal train to Harbour Halt.
  2. Walk down the hill to the quay.

As the afternoon light turns gold, the harbour empties out again. The boats are tied up, the market is hosed down, and the only sound is the water slapping gently against the stones of the quay, just as it has for centuries.
